Sheep have horizontal, rectangular pupils that give them a field of view of roughly 270–320 degrees, so they can watch for predators without turning their heads.
Sheep can recognize at least 50 individual sheep faces and remember them for up to two years.
In a 2017 Cambridge study, sheep learned to recognize celebrities like Barack Obama and Emma Watson from photographs.
Shrek, a Merino in New Zealand, avoided shearing for six years by hiding in caves. When he was finally shorn in 2004, his fleece weighed 27 kg (60 lb).
In 2015, an overgrown Australian sheep named Chris was shorn of a world-record 41.1 kg (90 lb) fleece.
Sheep have no upper front teeth. They tear grass using their lower teeth against a tough dental pad.
Sheep are ruminants with a four-chambered stomach: the rumen, reticulum, omasum and abomasum.
Dolly the sheep, born in Scotland on 5 July 1996, was the first mammal cloned from an adult cell. She was cloned from a mammary cell and named after Dolly Parton.
There are over a billion sheep in the world.
New Zealand has several sheep for every person living there.
Sheep were among the first animals humans domesticated, around 11,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent.
The wild ancestor of domestic sheep is the mouflon.
Newborn lambs can usually stand and walk within minutes of being born.
Ewes recognize their own lambs by smell and by their bleat, and lambs can pick out their mother's call in a noisy flock.
A group of sheep is called a flock, a herd, or in Australia and New Zealand, a mob.
A male sheep is a ram (or tup), a female is a ewe, and a castrated male is a wether.
Sheep have scent glands in front of their eyes and between their toes.
The lanolin in sheep's wool is used in lip balms, skin creams and cosmetics.
Most wild sheep shed their coats naturally, but many domestic breeds were bred to keep growing wool and must be shorn.
A sheep can get "cast": stuck on its back and unable to get up. This can be fatal if no one helps it.
Sheep with intestinal parasites have been observed eating plants high in tannins, which may be a form of self-medication.
Sheep can read human facial expressions and prefer a smiling face to an angry one.
The first passengers in a hot air balloon, in 1783 at Versailles, were a sheep named Montauciel, a duck and a rooster.
Jacob sheep can grow two, four or even six horns.
Barbados Blackbelly sheep are "hair sheep": they grow hair instead of wool and never need shearing.
For centuries, Spain banned the export of Merino sheep to protect its valuable wool trade.
Bighorn rams settle disputes by running at each other and clashing horns at around 32 km/h (20 mph).
Wool is naturally flame-resistant and tends to smolder instead of catching fire.
Shepherds in northern England once counted sheep with old number words like "yan, tan, tethera, methera, pimp."
The name of the Faroe Islands likely comes from Old Norse for "Sheep Islands."
Sheep are prey animals that flock for safety. A sheep on its own quickly becomes stressed.
A sheep's normal body temperature is about 39°C (102°F), warmer than a human's.
A ewe is pregnant for about five months, or roughly 147 days.
Twin lambs are common, and some breeds regularly have triplets.
The Soay sheep of St Kilda, Scotland, are a feral, primitive breed that scientists have studied for decades.
In 2005 in Turkey, about 1,500 sheep followed one another off a cliff while their shepherds ate breakfast. Around 450 of them died.
Wool can absorb up to about 30% of its own weight in moisture without feeling wet.
Sheep have a split upper lip, which lets them graze very close to the ground.
The argali is the largest wild sheep. Rams can weigh over 140 kg (300 lb).
Roquefort, feta and pecorino are traditionally made from sheep's milk.
Solar farms often use flocks of sheep to keep grass and weeds under control. This is called "solar grazing."
Sheep can learn to find their way through mazes and remember the route weeks later.
On unfenced hills, flocks are "hefted": ewes teach their lambs the boundaries of their home range, passing that knowledge down for generations.
Sheep horns never shed and keep growing for life. You can estimate a ram's age by counting the growth rings.
Domestic sheep have 54 chromosomes.
Each sheep has its own bleat, and flockmates can tell each other apart by voice.
Sheep can hear higher frequencies than humans.
